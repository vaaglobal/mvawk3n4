LESSON 01 · APPOINTMENT SCHEDULING

Appointment Scheduling

Scheduling is the backbone of a medical practice. As a VA, you control patient flow. Done well, it reduces no-shows, improves care access, and keeps providers productive.

The 5 principles of great medical scheduling
1. Triage before booking
Understand why the patient is calling before choosing a slot. New patient? Follow-up? Urgent? Each needs different booking logic.
2. Two-choice rule
Offer exactly two time slots. Open-ended "when are you free?" wastes time. Two choices respect the patient's autonomy efficiently.
3. Confirm everything
Verbally repeat: date, time, provider, location/telehealth link, and any prep instructions before ending the call.
4. Know urgency flags
Chest pain, confusion, severe bleeding, difficulty breathing → never schedule. Redirect to ER/911 immediately.
5. Document every change
Every booking, cancellation, reschedule must be logged with timestamp and reason in the EHR or scheduling system.

The 7-step scheduling workflow
1
Greet & identify the patient
Use name + DOB minimum. Never access records until identity is confirmed. "Could I get your full name and date of birth?"
2
Determine the reason for the visit
Ask: "What brings you in today?" or "What would you like to be seen for?" This determines appointment type, duration needed, and urgency level.
3
Triage urgency
Listen for red-flag symptoms: chest pain, difficulty breathing, numbness, bleeding, confusion, high fever. If present — stop and redirect to ER or 911. Never schedule emergencies.
4
Check referral & prior auth
For specialist visits: confirm the referral exists in the system. Check if the insurer requires prior authorization for this visit type. Flag if pending.
5
Verify insurance eligibility
Run real-time eligibility check in your EHR or clearinghouse. Confirm: in-network status, active coverage, copay amount, any deductible remaining.
6
Offer two time slots
Match slot duration to appointment type. Offer morning and afternoon options when possible. If scheduling a new patient, send intake paperwork immediately after booking.
7
Confirm & document
Verbally repeat all details. Log the booking in the EHR with timestamp and any special notes. Send confirmation via patient's preferred channel (text/email).
Case studies
CASE 01SCHEDULING ERRORHIGH STAKES
The Misrouted Cardiology Patient
WHAT HAPPENED

A VA at a busy clinic received a call from a 58-year-old patient complaining of "chest tightness and feeling tired." The VA, focused on clearing the call queue, scheduled a standard follow-up appointment for the next week without asking further questions.

THE PROBLEM

The patient had classic signs of unstable angina — a cardiac emergency. By the time the patient came to the follow-up a week later, they had suffered a minor heart attack two days prior and gone to the ER on their own.

WHAT SHOULD HAVE HAPPENED
✓
Ask follow-up triage questions: "How long have you had chest tightness? Does it spread to your arm or jaw? Are you short of breath?"
✓
Any combination of chest pain + fatigue in a patient over 50 = urgent clinical assessment, not routine scheduling
✓
Redirect: "I need to have a nurse speak with you right now before we schedule anything. Please hold." or "Please call 911 or go to the nearest ER."
LESSONScheduling is a clinical function. A VA who books incorrectly can delay life-saving care. Always triage before you type.

No-show & cancellation management

No-shows cost clinics thousands per week. As a VA, your reminder calls and follow-up processes directly impact revenue and patient outcomes.

48h
First reminder call or text
Confirm appointment, ask if they still plan to attend. Offer to reschedule if needed. Log the contact attempt.
24h
Second reminder
Automated text/email with date, time, provider, location, and any prep reminders. Include cancellation policy.
2h
Same-day check (high-risk patients)
For chronic condition patients (diabetes, hypertension) or post-procedure follow-ups, a personal call adds significant no-show reduction.
WHEN A PATIENT NO-SHOWSDocument the no-show in the EHR with timestamp. Attempt to call within 2 hours. For clinical follow-ups, inform the provider. Some clinics require a nurse callback for missed chronic disease appointments.
REMINDER CALL SCRIPT "Hello, may I speak with [Patient Name]? This is [Your Name] calling from [Clinic Name]. I'm calling to confirm your appointment with [Dr. Name] on [Date] at [Time]. [Pause for confirmation] Great! Please remember to [bring insurance card / fast from midnight / arrive 15 mins early]. If you need to reschedule, please call us at least 24 hours before. Is there anything you'd like me to note for the doctor before your visit?"

LESSON 02 · PATIENT INTAKE FORMS

Patient Intake Forms

Intake forms are the foundation of accurate clinical records and smooth billing. A VA must know what to collect, how to collect it, and what to do when information is missing or inconsistent.

Why intake accuracy matters
Clinical safety
Missing allergy or medication information can lead to dangerous drug interactions during treatment or prescribing.
Billing accuracy
Wrong insurance ID, member name, or group number leads to claim denials and delayed payments — often weeks of rework.
Legal compliance
Patient consent, privacy notices (HIPAA), and authorization forms must be completed and signed before services are rendered.

Case study: The billing nightmare from a typo
CASE 03BILLING ERROR
One Wrong Digit — Three Weeks of Rejected Claims
WHAT HAPPENED

A VA typed the patient's insurance member ID as "BCB004424" instead of "BCB004423" — one digit off. The patient was seen and the clinic submitted three weeks of claims before realizing every one had been rejected. The billing team spent 6 hours correcting and resubmitting.

ROOT CAUSE

The VA entered the number from memory after a phone call instead of scanning the card or asking the patient to spell it out. No verification step was in place.

PREVENTION PROTOCOL
✓
Always ask the patient to spell out their member ID digit by digit: "Could you read out each digit of your member ID for me?"
✓
Run an eligibility check immediately after entry — an incorrect ID will return an error from the insurer's portal
✓
Scan both sides of the insurance card and attach to the patient's digital record
✗
Never enter insurance information from memory or a previous call note — always re-verify

LESSON 03 · DOCUMENTATION

Documentation

Accurate, timely documentation protects patients, providers, and your career. Learn the types of clinical notes, documentation standards, and how to write clearly without practising medicine.

Types of documentation a medical VA handles
SOAP Notes (transcription)
Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan. The standard format used by providers. VAs may transcribe from dictation but never write the clinical assessment or plan themselves.
Referral Letters
Sent from PCP to specialist summarizing patient's reason for referral, relevant history, and current medications. VA prepares draft from template — provider reviews and signs.
After-Visit Summaries (AVS)
Given to patients after appointments. Includes diagnosis, instructions, follow-up date, and medications. VA formats and prints/sends — never edits the clinical content.
Prior Authorization Requests
Documentation sent to insurers to justify a procedure or medication. VA compiles clinical history, diagnosis codes, and supporting notes. Provider must approve before submission.
Phone Triage Notes
Every patient call must be documented in the EHR: who called, time, reason, what was said, and outcome. This is your legal record of the interaction.
Prescription Refill Requests
VA receives refill request, verifies patient identity and medication name/dosage in chart, routes to provider for approval. Never refill without clinical sign-off.
Understanding SOAP notes — with a worked example

A VA may transcribe SOAP notes from audio dictation. You must understand each section so you transcribe it correctly — even if you never write the clinical parts yourself.

S — SUBJECTIVEWhat the patient reports: symptoms, complaints, duration. "Patient reports…" Written in the patient's own words. VA transcribes exactly as dictated.
O — OBJECTIVEMeasurable findings: vitals, lab results, physical exam findings. Numbers and clinical observations. VA transcribes exactly — never rounds numbers or paraphrases.
A — ASSESSMENTThe provider's clinical diagnosis or impression. VA NEVER WRITES THIS. Transcribe only from provider dictation, word for word.
P — PLANTreatment plan: medications, referrals, follow-ups, tests ordered. VA NEVER WRITES THIS. Transcribe only. Flag anything unclear for provider review.
SOAP NOTE EXAMPLE — PATIENT: CHIDI NWOSU S — SUBJECTIVE Patient reports persistent cough for 10 days, worsening at night. Reports mild fever (self-measured 38.2°C at home). Denies chest pain or shortness of breath. No known sick contacts. O — OBJECTIVE Temp: 38.1°C · BP: 122/78 · HR: 88 · SpO2: 98% on room air. Lungs: mild bilateral crackles on auscultation. Throat: mild erythema, no exudate. No lymphadenopathy. A — ASSESSMENT Acute bronchitis, likely viral. R/O early pneumonia. P — PLAN 1. Chest X-ray ordered. 2. Supportive care: fluids, rest, honey/lemon. 3. Prescribed amoxicillin 500mg TID x 7 days if X-ray confirms bacterial infection. 4. Follow-up in 5 days or sooner if symptoms worsen.
Documenting a phone call — the right way

Every call with a patient must be documented. This is your legal record. "If it wasn't documented, it didn't happen."

✓ GOOD DOCUMENTATION
  • Specific and objective: "Patient called at 10:42am reporting headache x 3 days, rated 7/10, no vision changes, taking ibuprofen with partial relief."
  • States the action taken: "Message sent to Dr. Adeyemi's nurse per clinic triage protocol. Patient advised to seek ER if symptoms worsen or new symptoms develop."
  • Documents who, what, when, outcome
  • Uses medical abbreviations correctly and consistently
  • Notes if patient declined offered action
✗ POOR DOCUMENTATION
  • "Patient called about headache. Told them to take meds." — vague, no clinical detail, no action documented
  • "Patient seems fine." — VA is making a clinical judgment
  • Missing timestamp
  • Copy-pasting from a previous note without updating specifics
  • No documentation at all — most common and most dangerous error
PHONE CALL NOTE TEMPLATE Date/Time: [MM/DD/YYYY HH:MM AM/PM] Contact Type: [Inbound call / Outbound call / Voicemail left] Patient Name: [Full Name] | DOB: [DOB] Caller identity verified: [Yes / No — method used] Reason for contact: [Patient's own words or brief summary] Symptoms reported (if applicable): [Symptom, duration, severity 1-10, associated symptoms] Action taken: [Message forwarded to provider / Appointment scheduled / Referred to ER / Information provided] Patient instructions given: [Exact instructions told to patient] Patient response: [Patient agreed / Patient declined / No answer — voicemail left] Documented by: [VA Name, Title]

LESSON 04 · INSURANCE VERIFICATION

Insurance Verification

Insurance verification is one of the highest-impact tasks a medical VA performs. Getting it right prevents claim denials, patient billing shocks, and provider revenue loss.

The complete 6-step verification process
1
Collect insurance information from patient
Obtain: insurance company name, member ID, group number, policy holder name and DOB. Ask them to spell out the member ID digit by digit. Scan both sides of the card if in-person or via portal.
2
Access the insurer's eligibility portal or call payer line
Most clinics use a clearinghouse (Availity, Change Healthcare) or direct payer portal. Enter patient details and run a real-time eligibility check. For urgent cases, call the payer's provider line directly.
3
Verify active coverage & effective dates
Confirm the plan is active and the effective date covers the appointment date. Check termination date — coverage can lapse between employer changes or unpaid premiums.
4
Confirm network status & benefits
Is the provider in-network? What is the copay? What is the remaining deductible? Are there visit limits (e.g. 20 PT visits/year)? Is a referral required for this specialty?
5
Check prior authorization requirements
Certain procedures (MRI, CT, surgery, specialty drugs) require pre-approval. Run the CPT code against the payer's prior auth list. If required, initiate the request immediately — some take 5–10 business days.
6
Document & communicate results
Enter all verification details in the EHR: payer name, rep name (if called), reference number, copay confirmed, auth status. Inform the billing team of any flags. Inform the patient of their expected out-of-pocket cost.

Case studies
CASE 05PRIOR AUTH FAILURE
The $4,200 MRI That Nobody Pre-Approved
WHAT HAPPENED

A doctor ordered an MRI for a patient's knee injury. The scheduling VA booked the MRI for the following week without checking prior auth requirements. The scan was performed, the patient was billed, and the insurer denied the entire $4,200 claim because prior authorization was never obtained.

CONSEQUENCES
✗
The clinic had to write off $4,200 as a bad debt — the patient couldn't pay, insurance wouldn't cover it
✗
The patient was shocked and distressed — they thought they were covered
✗
The VA received a formal disciplinary warning
CORRECT PROCESS
✓
Before scheduling any imaging: check the payer's prior auth requirement list for the CPT code (e.g. MRI knee = CPT 73721)
✓
If prior auth is required: submit the authorization request with the clinical notes supporting medical necessity
✓
Only schedule the procedure after receiving written authorization with an auth number
✓
Document the auth number in the EHR and on the order
